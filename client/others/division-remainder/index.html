Sharing oranges at school

You have 13 oranges. You want to share them equally among 4 friends. How many does each friend get — and how many are left over that cannot be shared?

💡 The concept

What is a remainder?

When you divide and the number does not split perfectly, the leftover part is called the remainder. In maths we write: 13 ÷ 4 = 3 remainder 1. The remainder is never bigger than the divisor.

🕐 The story

Reading a clock

It is 26 hours since midnight. What time does the clock show? Hours on a clock cycle every 12. So we need to know: what is 26 when wrapped around a cycle of 12? The answer is 2 o'clock — because 26 mod 12 = 2.

💡 The concept

Modulo — the cycling operation

The modulo operation ( mod or % ) gives only the remainder. It wraps numbers around a cycle. Every clock, calendar, and rotating system uses this idea without calling it modulo.

🔢 The story

Odd and even — the simplest test

You arrange chairs in pairs for a classroom. If every chair pairs up with no leftover, the number is even. If one chair is always alone at the end, the number is odd. This is just asking: what is N mod 2?

💡 The concept

Remainder as a classifier

The remainder does not just measure leftover — it classifies. N mod 2 = 0 means even. N mod 2 = 1 means odd. Any number mod any value creates a classification system. This is the engine behind sorting, filtering, and routing in code.

The lock that uses remainder

You and a friend agree: to send a secret message, multiply the code number by 7, then divide by 26 and keep only the remainder. The remainder maps to a letter. A becomes B, B becomes I... This is the Caesar cipher — one of history's first encryption systems.

💡 The concept

Modulo in patterns and security

When you apply mod to a sequence, the remainders form a repeating, predictable pattern. But when that modulus is a prime number, the pattern becomes hard to reverse — which is the foundation of cryptography and internet security.

✍️ The story

Writing the logic yourself

You have now seen remainder in sharing, cycling, classifying, and encoding. Before any real code, a good thinker writes the logic in plain language first — this is called pseudocode. It bridges your thinking and the machine's language.

💡 The concept

Pseudocode — thinking out loud

Pseudocode is not a real programming language. It has no syntax to memorize. It is your logic written clearly enough that anyone — or any AI — could turn it into real code. The skill of writing good pseudocode is the same skill as writing a good AI prompt.

23// Step 5 — combining all ideas: page calculator
24FUNCTION paginateItems(totalItems, perPage):
